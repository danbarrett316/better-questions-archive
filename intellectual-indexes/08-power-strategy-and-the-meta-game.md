# Power, Strategy, and the Meta-Game

The game-theoretic and strategic dimension of Barrett's thinking. Encompasses common knowledge games, the metagame (the game about the game), status transactions, information cascades, and the ethical imperative for good people to acquire power rather than cede it. This theme evolves from theoretical game theory applied to COVID-19 into a practical framework for navigating competitive dynamics in PPC, business, and life.

---

## Core Concepts

### The Common Knowledge Game
- **Common knowledge:** Not just what we know, but what we know others know, and what we know others know we know.
- Public statements create common knowledge even when all information was already privately available.
- The blue-eyed islanders riddle (Terence Tao): the "missionary" — the public statement — triggers a cascade of action that private knowledge could not.
- "Common Knowledge is created by public statement, even when all the information was already available privately." ("Missionaries," 2020)

### The Missionary
- The source of common knowledge — the entity whose public statements create shared understanding.
- Media outlets, the Federal Reserve, industry thought leaders, Google's own communications.
- "You better be aware of who the missionaries are, and what they're saying."
- When missionaries spread false information, the common knowledge game accelerates irrationality.
- ("Missionaries," 2020)

### The Keynesian Beauty Contest
- First-order thinking: choose based on your preference.
- Second-order thinking: choose based on what you think others prefer.
- Third-order thinking: choose based on what you think others think others prefer.
- "Whenever we shift from playing the game to playing the player, we have made the move from statistical analysis to game theory." ("Monty Hall," 2020)
- ("The Beauty Contest," 2020)

### Playing the Meta-Game
A three-part framework for understanding competitive dynamics:

**Part 1 — Information Cascades and Equilibria:**
- Information cascades: when people make decisions based on observing others rather than on private information.
- Markets and industries fall into equilibria — states where no one benefits from changing strategy.
- Equilibria can be suboptimal but stable because changing first is risky.
- Connection to PPC: the industry falls into equilibria around bidding strategies, match types, and campaign structures that may not be optimal but are self-reinforcing.

**Part 2 — Fear, Deming, and Mob Behavior:**
- Deming's "Drive Out Fear": fear makes people conform to norms rather than innovate.
- Mob behavior: individuals in groups behave differently than individuals alone.
- Power through becoming "expensive to deal with" (Michael Korda).
- "With numbers there are no laws." (Bill Buford, *Among the Thugs*)

**Part 3 — Status, Signaling, and Competitive Advantage:**
- Competitive advantage comes from doing what others cannot or will not do.
- In PPC: alpha generation through conversion event shifting, mid-funnel proxies, and deliberate algorithmic arbitrage.
- Alpha erodes over time as competitors adapt.

### Status as a Learnable Game (Keith Johnstone)
- Three player types: compulsive high-status, compulsive low-status, and the status expert.
- The status expert reads the room and adjusts.
- See-saw principle: position status just slightly above your partner's.
- Status comes from behavior, and behavior can be learned.
- "If you have an ethical belief about the world, it is incumbent upon you to do something about it." — Which requires allies, which requires status, which requires learning the game.
- Kind people often opt out of power entirely — Barrett calls this "cope."
- ("Power Games," 2025)

### The Ethics of Power
- Ethical people must not shy away from power. Ceding power to the unethical does not make you virtuous.
- "If you want to impact the world in any way, you will need allies; and if you need allies, you need status."
- "There are no points for being on the right side of history. All that matters is what you do."
- ("Power Games," 2025)

### Mob Irrationality and Social Pressure
- Irrationality spreads because it is too costly for individuals to resist.
- "You can gain power by becoming expensive to deal with." (Michael Korda, *Power*)
- Social media as a Skinner Box rewarding worst tendencies.
- Ionesco's *Rhinoceros*: the gradual normalization of irrationality. "Nowhere is it written that it must be so."
- ("Rhinoceros," 2020)

### The War of Attrition (PPC Application)
- Google Ads as a competitive game where alpha (returns above the market benchmark) is generated by identifying and exploiting gaps.
- The algorithmic bidding paradox: as more competitors adopt the same algorithmic tools, returns converge to zero.
- Winning requires doing what others cannot or will not do — knowledge creation, not tool adoption.
- (Agency Steering Document; PPC Philosophy)
