# Mortality, Impermanence, and Existential Freedom

A surprisingly deep existential thread running through the blog, arguing that death and limitation are not obstacles but the very conditions that make meaning possible. Barrett consistently returns to the insight that the self is transient, emotions are temporary, and constraint — including the ultimate constraint of mortality — is what gives life shape and significance.

---

## Core Concepts

### Death as the Background of Responsibility
- "Death forms the background against which our act of being becomes a responsibility." (Viktor Frankl)
- Without the constraints of limited time and resources, we lose willingness and motivation to focus — and without focus, nothing of worth is achieved.
- "It is exactly that which you let go which gives your life meaning." ("This, Or That," 2021)
- The immortality thought experiment: if you could live forever, every choice would become meaningless — you could eventually do everything, making nothing matter.

### Constraint as Liberation
- Limitation is not a defect but the source of meaning.
- **Parkinson's Law:** Work expands to fill available time. Deadlines and constraints compress work into productive intensity.
- Constraint forces choice, and choice is where meaning lives.
- Applied to PPC: the ideal state is when budget is the constraint — something you control. When the constraint moves to something outside your control, you lose agency.
- ("This, Or That," 2021; PPC Philosophy; "Just One Man," 2025)

### The Impermanent Self
- Journal entries from months ago feel like they were written by a stranger.
- "The 'you' of last week is not the 'you' of today."
- Emotions are overwhelming but completely temporary.
- "There is no you; that what you think of as 'you' is simply a narrative born from linked impressions; that your consciousness is multiple parts, assumed to be a whole simply because they're all contained inside the same skin." ("If I Could Tell You Anything," 2025)
- "Even in the darkest of times, we can get better" — because the person suffering today will not exist tomorrow.
- ("The Man I've Become," 2021; "If I Could Tell You Anything," 2025)

### The Constructed Reality
- The brain literally constructs our perceived reality. Perceptual interpolation, the blind spot, the fact that photons have no color — "All the richness of life is created inside your head."
- "We spend our whole lives, in other words, living in a world that doesn't quite exist yet." (Bill Bryson)
- "Where body meets air, we are all cadavers." — At the surface level, our outer cells are already dead.
- ("Where Body Meets Air," 2021)

### Anxiety and Finitude
- "Anxiety is the absolute certainty that something bad is going to happen... Paired with the uncertainty of when." ("The Other Shoe," 2021)
- Human existence uniquely defined by awareness of limited time.
- **Gratitude as antidote:** Not sentimentality but existential necessity. "Gratitude for the Now — for the immediate, embodied moment — is the antidote for anxiety."
- "The day will never arrive when you finally have everything under control... Let's start by admitting defeat: None of this is ever going to happen." (Oliver Burkeman)
- ("The Other Shoe," 2021)

### Failure as Universal and Inevitable
- "Living, then, means failing. The only choice we have is whether we fail while trying to do something great... or fail by simple inaction, passivity, and cowardice."
- Failure feels like a death sentence because it is a proxy for death anxiety.
- **Liminality:** Nature is gradient, not binary — "a smooth transition between states, in constant flux, neither one thing nor the other, but both and neither."
- All-or-nothing thinking (the cognitive distortion of pure success/failure) is the psychological trap that systems design circumvents.
- ("All Or Nothing," 2021)

### Resilience and Rebuilding
- "Somedays, it's just your turn." — A personal mantra for accepting that bad days happen regardless of preparation. Entropy enters the system. Things fall apart.
- "But it'll be better tomorrow. And when you wake up? You give those bastards hell." ("Somedays, It's Just Your Turn," 2021)
- "How can you build something if you don't know what it's supposed to be? ... One piece at a time." ("The Elephants," 2021)
- "Even in the darkest of times, we can get better." — Earned through personal testimony of surviving a suicide attempt and rebuilding.

### Radical Freedom (Sartre / Existentialism)
- "Everything is a choice; not choosing is also a choice."
- Self-respect (Joan Didion) as the only reliable internal metric.
- "Pay it and live, or run from yourself until you die." ("Everything Is A Choice," 2022)
- "No one's coming. No keys to the city, no check." — You must act on your own behalf. ("Wanting To Be Wanted," 2022)
- "You are fighting for your life. Did you forget?" ("Act Now," 2022)

### Writing, Creation, and Mortality
- "A picture can't breathe. Words can." — Words change when you do; they capture the living quality of experience.
- The question of why create when AI can produce text is answered through the irreducible value of lived, felt, human expression.
- ("Why Do It At All?," 2025)

## Key Thinkers

| Thinker | Framework | Key Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Viktor Frankl** | Logotherapy | Meaning through constraint and suffering; death as the background of responsibility |
| **Jean-Paul Sartre** | Existentialism | Radical freedom; bad faith; everything is a choice |
| **Joan Didion** | "On Self-Respect" | Self-respect as doing things you don't want to do; putting fears aside |
| **Oliver Burkeman** | Four Thousand Weeks | Finitude as the human condition; admitting defeat to find peace |
| **Albert Camus** | The Myth of Sisyphus (implicit) | The Sisyphean struggle; the boulder metaphor; "one must imagine Sisyphus happy" |
| **Ernest Becker** | The Denial of Death | Death anxiety as the driver of human behavior; "the sadist finds the masochist ready-made" |
| **Bill Bryson** | The Body | The constructed nature of perception; "where body meets air, we are all cadavers" |

## Connections to Other Themes

- **Behavioral Systems:** "Could I do this forever?" is a sustainability question rooted in mortality awareness; Barrett's Rule addresses the finitude of willpower
- **Self-Acceptance:** The trance of unworthiness is intensified by death anxiety; self-acceptance is the existential antidote
- **Risk/Uncertainty:** Death as the ultimate risk of ruin; finitude as the context for all decision-making
- **Empathy:** Epistemic humility and empathy flow from recognizing our shared mortality and fallibility
- **Systems Thinking:** Entropy enters every system; the work is never permanently done; constraints are the condition of all meaning
