# Behavioral Systems and Personal Change

The personal application of systems thinking to human behavior. The single most dominant theme across the Better Questions blog: **personal outcomes are primarily products of the systems we operate within rather than failures of individual willpower or character.** The framework rejects self-blame as counterproductive and replaces it with system design.

---

## Core Concepts

### Barrett's Rule: "No Goals Without Systems"
The formal statement of the blog's core principle. Goals without systems are wishes. The default reality is dictated by systems, not willpower.

- "Our default reality is dictated by our systems."
- "When you accept that the problem is often not internal, but rather an issue of the systems you find yourself embedded within, you approach the problems of success and failure differently."
- "You start viewing your problems as largely mechanical."
- ("Barrett's Rule," 2021)

### The Five Behavioral Systems
Five types of systems that can be designed to support behavior change:
1. **Pre-commitment:** Committing in advance to a course of action before temptation arises.
2. **Stakes:** Creating real consequences (financial, social) for failure to follow through.
3. **Friction:** Making undesired behavior harder and desired behavior easier.
4. **Feedback:** Creating loops that make progress visible and adjustable.
5. **Social Pressure:** Enlisting others to hold you accountable.
- ("Barrett's Rule," 2021; "The Product Is You," 2021)

### Systems Over Self-Blame
- Self-blame is a paradoxical assertion of autonomy: "I am all powerful, therefore this is all my fault."
- Accepting weakness — acknowledging that you cannot simply will yourself to change — is the precondition for progress.
- "A bad system will beat a good person every time." (W. Edwards Deming) — Barrett's single most-cited quote.
- ("Barrett's Rule," 2021; Gemba Walk, 2021; LTP Introduction, 2021)

### Subtraction Over Addition (Via Negativa)
- Happiness and improvement come more from removing what harms than from adding what pleases.
- "If true wealth consists in worriless sleeping, clear conscience, reciprocal gratitude, absence of envy, good appetite, muscle strength, physical energy, frequent laughs, no meals alone, no gym class, some physical labor, good bowel movements, no meeting rooms, and periodic surprises, then it is largely subtractive." (Taleb, Antifragile)
- "Better Than Your Worst": Bigger gains come from raising the floor (eliminating worst performances) than raising the ceiling (trying to beat personal bests). ("Better Than Your Worst," 2021)
- ("The Product Is You," 2021; "The Most Powerful Chart in the World," 2021)

### Ironic Process Theory and Priming
- **Ironic Process Theory** (Dr. Martin Wegner): Suppressing a thought makes it stronger because the mind must imagine it to check if you're thinking about it. "We are entwined with what we struggle against."
- **Priming** (Clifton Mitchell): The solution is not suppression but redirection. A four-step process: (1) define undesired behavior, (2) define its positive opposite, (3) create a present-tense statement, (4) repeat hundreds of times daily.
- "The mind cannot consciously avoid, it can only intentionally focus and attend."
- ("Ironic Processes," 2021; "Dominant Thoughts," 2021)

### Maximum Achievability vs. Maximum Sustainability
- What you *can* do vs. what you can do *forever*.
- "Could I do this forever?" — the sustainability test.
- Good behavior compounds over time, making time a critical factor.
- Temporary intensity within a sustainable overall framework is acceptable; unsustainable intensity is not.
- ("Sustainably Successful," 2021)

### The Gemba Walk (Process, Not People)
- Adapted from Taiichi Ohno / Toyota Production System.
- "Go to the Gemba" — go to where the work actually happens. Observe directly.
- "Process, not people" — when something goes wrong, examine the process, not the individual.
- Asking "why" repeatedly to trace from symptoms to root causes.
- ("The Real Place," 2021)

### Root Cause Analysis: Proximal vs. Distal Causes
- **Proximal cause:** The event immediately preceding an effect.
- **Distal cause:** The deeper, further-removed triggering event in a chain.
- The brain's bias toward proximal attribution masks the true root cause.
- "Training yourself to look backwards in the chain of causation, towards the root cause of your problems, will make you better at everything you do... and make your progress seem effortless." ("Quarterly Review Part 1: Proximal and Distal Causes," 2021)
- The diet-mood-parenting example: irritability with children was proximally attributed to their behavior but was actually caused distally by diet choices.

### The Quarterly Review System
A structured self-reflection practice:
1. **Round Up:** Gather data (journals, metrics, notes).
2. **Proximal/Distal Analysis:** Trace problems to root causes.
3. **Feedback Loop Assessment:** Identify reinforcing and stabilizing loops.
4. **Stabilization:** Recognize when plateaus are natural and expected.
5. **Planning:** Set direction based on analysis, not aspiration.

### The Personal Danban (Kanban)
- Three "Iron Laws": Visualize Your Work, Limit Your Work In Progress, Ignore Local Optima.
- "Existential overhead": the anxiety of keeping tasks in your head.
- Kanban boards with columns: To Do, Doing, The Pen, Done.
- Physical space as a behavioral constraint — tasks need to fit on the board.
- The system as a "tool for self-reflection; a method of making our choices manifest so that we can interrogate them with our whole selves."
- ("Personal Danban Parts 1-2," 2021)

### Order of Operations
- A biological hierarchy that must be addressed in sequence: **Sleep → Food → Attention → Focus**.
- No amount of optimization at higher levels works if lower levels are broken.
- ("Order of Operations," 2022)

### Choosing Your Vices
- The sustainable heuristic: rather than eliminating all vices, consciously choose which ones to keep.
- Abstinence as often less sustainable than managed indulgence.
- ("Choosing Your Vices," 2022)

## Connections to Other Themes

- **Systems Thinking:** Barrett's Rule is the personal instantiation of systems thinking — the same principles that govern factories and ecosystems govern individual behavior
- **SPC/Variation:** Feedback loops require measurement; the Quarterly Review is a personal SPC process
- **Mortality/Existential:** "Could I do this forever?" only matters because time is finite; sustainability is an existential imperative
- **Self-Acceptance:** Systems over self-blame directly connects to the "trance of unworthiness" — the insight that you are not broken, your systems are
- **Empathy/Communication:** "Process, not people" extends to relationships — conflicts are systemic, not personal
